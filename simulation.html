Ansys 2026 R1 FEA Studies

NEOGUARD — SIMULATION RESULTS

This section presents five finite element analysis (FEA) results associated with the proposed NeoGuard textile design simulated in Ansys 2026 R1 Mechanical. The studies evaluate transient warming response, coupled thermo-structural loads, total structural deformation, and equivalent von-Mises thermal stress.

B: Transient Thermal — Temperature (Ansys 2026 R1 • sm1.jpeg)

What the Image Shows

A 3D finite element mesh of the multilayer textile slab showing transient temperature distribution after 120 seconds of continuous heating from an initial ambient baseline.

Key Observation

The core heating area reaches a maximum temperature of 37.276 °C at 120s, exactly conforming to the target neonatal thermal band (36.5°C to 37.5°C). Outer boundary edges remain buffered at 25.488 °C.

Relevance to NeoGuard Textile Design

Validates the warm-up kinetics of the heating layer, demonstrating rapid yet smooth heating that reaches safe clinical temperatures in 2 minutes without dangerous thermal overshoot.

Contour map of total physical deflection (in millimeters) resulting from thermal expansion across the textile layers under operating temperatures.

What the Image Shows

Finite element equivalent stress distribution (von Mises criteria in MPa) induced by thermal expansion gradients across the textile volume.

Key Observation

The maximum equivalent stress is only 0.53586 MPa, which is far below the tensile and yield thresholds of textile fibers (typically 15 to 50+ MPa), indicating a high safety factor.

Relevance to NeoGuard Textile Design

Confirms that repetitive thermal cycling will not cause material fatigue, fiber rupture, or shear detachment of integrated sensor leads over prolonged use.

Note on Ansys Simulation Methodology & Prototype Status

The finite element analysis (FEA) results presented above were simulated using Ansys 2026 R1 Mechanical for the NeoGuard multilayer textile structure. The simulations encompass steady-state thermal, transient thermal (120s warm-up), and coupled static structural analyses (total deformation and equivalent von-Mises stress). All images preserve original aspect ratios, result scales, and min/max markers. These numerical simulations support physical prototype engineering and require laboratory bench and clinical validation.
